Field Trips at Elachee
Wild Encounters & Unforgettable Fun
You Want a Field Trip That’s More Than Just a Day Away
Elachee makes it easy to give your students a learning experience that’s meaningful, memorable, and rooted in the real world.
What We Offer
Field trips designed to meet Georgia Standards of Excellence (GSE)
Programs tailored by age group and grade level
Explore habitats, observe wildlife, study ecosystems — on-site in the Chicopee Woods
Engage with native reptiles, amphibians, and more
Flexible time slots, organized check-in, and staff support to help your trip run smoothly
Popular Topics Include:
Students explore Georgia’s native wildlife while learning how animals survive, adapt, and depend on one another. Hands-on experiences bring life science concepts to life in a memorable way.
Grades: Pre-K–2 | 3–5 | 6–8
From daytime sky patterns to stars and planets, students explore Earth’s place in the universe through interactive demonstrations and observation-based learning.
Grades: K–2 | 3–5 | 6–8
Seasonal availability (Nov–Feb).
Students investigate rocks, minerals, fossils, and landforms to understand how Earth’s surface changes over time. Programs emphasize inquiry, observation, and real-world connections.
Grades: K–2 | 3–5 | 6–8
Students learn about Georgia’s Indigenous cultures and their deep connection to the land. History, ecology, and cultural understanding come together through place-based learning.
Grades: 3–5 | 6–8
Through hands-on exploration, students discover how plants grow, reproduce, and support life. Lessons connect plant science to ecosystems students see every day.
Grades: Pre-K–2 | 3–5 | 6–8
Students explore watersheds, aquatic ecosystems, and the water cycle while learning why clean water matters. Programs encourage environmental responsibility and systems thinking.
Grades: K–2 | 3–5 | 6–8 | 9–12
Some programs are seasonal (Mar–Nov).
Why It Matters
Every Elachee field trip supports our mission: to foster environmental understanding through education and experience.
Your visit helps fund conservation, animal care, and science education for thousands of students across Georgia.
And most importantly?
It reminds your students that nature isn’t something to memorize, it’s something to explore.
